Validation

julia --project -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.test()' runs the test suite (373 tests, ≈ 8 min). Among the tests:

  • Tracks reproduce the full reduction to < 2 m; the analytic and numerical limb crossings agree to 1 ms.
  • The exact projection of an ellipsoid equals the Dunham et al. (2019) formulas, and those of Magnusson (1986) with the phase shifted by 90°, to 10⁻⁹; the Haumea example (1161 × 852 × 513 km, aspect 76.3°) gives 852 × 569 km; mesh silhouettes agree to 0.5 % in area; the closed-form rotational amplitude equals the numerical one.
  • Synthetic events: timings, ellipse parameters, clock offsets (0.7 s), 3-D axes (340, 290, 250 km) and rotation phase from three events, mesh scale and phase, and the surface pressure of a 2002 XV93-like atmosphere (124 nbar) are recovered within their errors; the posterior widths agree with the covariance and with χ² profiles.
  • Ray tracing: the bending angle of an isothermal atmosphere agrees with ω ≈ −ν √(2πr/H) to 2 % (the expected O(H/r) correction); the free-molecular-flow limit ω = −πν is exact.
  • Real events: the Huya occultation of 2019 March 18 reproduces the published ellipse (Santos-Sanz et al., 2022) within its uncertainties; the five 2002 KX14 events (Rizos et al., 2025) reproduce the published geometry, semi-major axis and diameter within 1–2σ; the 2003 UY117 occultation (Kretlow et al., 2024) reproduces the published limb, χ²/dof and astrometry. For 2002 XV93 (Arimatsu et al., 2026), the ray-traced atmosphere reproduces the authors' model light curves to 0.01 rms, and the joint light-curve fit recovers their surface pressures for CH₄, N₂ and CO within about half their errors.
  • Generic engine: χ² profiles and confidence intervals at parameter bounds and for flat profiles; infinite σ for directions the data do not constrain; light curves with an event between two exposures or wholly inside it; negative chords crossed between their samples; sampler starts given explicitly.

The live services (MPC codes, Horizons, SBDB; using HTTP) were checked by hand and are not part of the automated tests. The real-event atmosphere results come from the 2002 XV93 example; the atmosphere tests use synthetic data.

Synthetic-data validation is no substitute for real events: compare with published reductions (e.g. SORA, Occult) before relying on new results.

Not implemented (yet)

Eccentric or inclined rings and radial ring profiles; central flashes beyond the spherical, transparent atmosphere model (haze, oblate atmospheres); limb topography models; light-curve readers for other programs (LiMovie, Occular); prediction services (occultation catalogue queries, Occult Watcher export); automatic differentiation of the atmosphere fits (these use finite differences).
