Binaries and contact binaries

Three limb models describe bodies that are not a single convex outline:

ModelShapeTypical use
BinaryLimb(primary, secondary)two separate bodies, each with its own limb modela body and its satellite; a binary system of similar bodies
ContactBinaryLimb()two overlapping ellipses along a common axis (2-D)a bilobed body seen in one event
ContactBinaryEllipsoids()two triaxial ellipsoids on the body x-axis, rotating (3-D)several events at different rotational phases, light-curve amplitudes

Their apparent limb is a CompositeOutline: the union of the outlines of the components, each with its own centre. The union is in general not star-shaped. A chord can cross it several times, and the boundary is not a radius per direction from a centre. Everything else (negative chords, clock offsets, priors, posteriors, multi-event fits) works as for single bodies.

Observations: chords with several drops

A chord that crosses two bodies, or both lobes of a contact binary with a gap between them, has several drops. Give its contacts as a vector:

Chord("Station 4", obs; contacts=[Contact(:immersion, t1, 0.05), Contact(:emersion, t2, 0.05),
                                  Contact(:immersion, t3, 0.05), Contact(:emersion, t4, 0.05)])

Immersions and emersions must alternate. occulted_intervals(chord) lists the drops, and plot(event) draws each of them.

Contact labels. A contact can be assigned to a component:

  • Contact(...; body=:primary) or body=:secondary for a BinaryLimb;
  • body=:lobe1 or body=:lobe2 for the contact-binary models.

A labelled contact is compared with the crossing of that component alone. An unlabelled contact (body=:auto, the default) is matched to the nearest crossing of the same kind (immersion or emersion) of the whole outline. Labels make the fit robust when a satellite is crossed by only one or two chords, or when the assignment is otherwise ambiguous.

Light curves with several drops. detect_occultations(lc; method=:threshold) finds the drops as runs of points below half the baseline; the default box search would find two deep drops with a short gap between them as one. Time each one in its own window with fit_timing(lc; tmin, tmax, immersion, emersion, …), between the neighbouring drops, as in the contact-binary example.

simulate_lightcurve and occultation_flux take a list of drops [(t_imm, t_emm), …]:

  • drops more than about ten Fresnel scales apart are independent;
  • closer drops are computed together. The complex amplitudes of the opaque strips add (Babinet's principle), so the light diffracted into a narrow gap between two lobes is part of the model.

The joint fit of the light curves (photometry=true) uses the same model. It is exact where the timing of each drop on its own is not: the timing treats the gap as fully lit.

The visible segments of the chords

Where a chord saw the star (before the first drop, between drops, after the last drop), no part of the body can have been. For the composite models these visible segments are constraints, just like negative chords. Each segment is shortened at every contact by 3σ of its timing (at least the chord's sensitivity).

ValueBehaviour
visible=:softpenalty for the depth by which the limb enters a segment, in units of sensitivity × speed
:hardno limb may enter a segment
:nonethe segments are ignored
:auto (the default):soft for the composite models; :none for single convex limbs, which cannot produce a drop where none was seen, so the published results are unchanged

The visible segments decide between solutions that the contacts alone allow:

  • a chord with two drops proves the gap between the lobes;
  • a satellite cannot grow across a chord that saw the star.

A body with a satellite, a binary system

r = fit_limb(ev; model=BinaryLimb())                              # ellipse + circle (default)
r = fit_limb(ev; model=BinaryLimb(EllipseLimb(), EllipseLimb()))  # an ellipse for the satellite too

Parameters.

  • The primary's parameters keep their names; center_f and center_g are the centre of the primary.
  • The secondary's parameters take the prefix sec_: sec_radius, or sec_equatorial_radius, sec_oblateness and sec_position_angle.
  • sep_f and sep_g [km] are the position of the secondary relative to the primary in each event (suffix _k in multi-event fits).

Starting values. The contacts of each event are split into two groups (by their labels, otherwise by clustering), and each group gives the starting values of one body.

Circle or ellipse for the satellite. The circle (three parameters) is the default: few chords usually cross a satellite. An ellipse has five parameters. It needs at least three chords on the satellite, better four or more, and the visible segments to exclude elongated solutions. With two chords it is undetermined: the fit reports a singular covariance. Compare the two with the Bayesian information criterion, χ² + k ln n.

Derived quantities.

  • those of each component;
  • size_ratio: the ratio of the area-equivalent radii;
  • mass_ratio: size_ratio³, for equal densities;
  • system_equivalent_radius;
  • per event: separation [km], separation_mas and separation_pa (position angle of the secondary from the primary).

Astrometry. astrometry(fit; reference=…) gives one of three positions:

referencePosition
:center (default)the centre of the primary
:barycenterequal densities
:photocenterequal albedos

For a satellite of a quarter of the primary's size, the three differ by several milliarcseconds at TNO distances. For an orbit fit of the system, use the barycentre.

The separation in each event is free. A Keplerian orbit of the secondary (and with it the mass of the system) over several events is a natural extension.

Contact binaries

In 2-D, ContactBinaryLimb has these parameters:

  • a1, b1, a2, b2: the lobes, with a along the common axis;
  • axis_pa: the position angle of the axis, from lobe 1 to lobe 2;
  • neck: the centres are d = (a1 + a2)(1 − neck) apart.
neckLobes
0the lobes touch
> 0the lobes overlap, with a waist
< 0there is a gap (down to −0.5)

The centre is the area-weighted centre of the lobes. Lobe 1 is always the larger one; the fit swaps the lobes and turns the axis by 180° if needed. The derived quantities are:

  • equivalent_radius (of the area of the union);
  • lobe1_equivalent_radius and lobe2_equivalent_radius;
  • size_ratio and mass_ratio;
  • separation;
  • length (along the axis);
  • neck_width (the width of the waist).

The starting values come from the ellipse fitted to all contacts, whose major axis gives the axis of the lobes.

In 3-D, ContactBinaryEllipsoids has two triaxial ellipsoids (a1, b1, c1, a2, b2, c2) with a neck as above. They rotate with pole_ra, pole_dec, W0 and period, as in EllipsoidLimb; center_f and center_g are the centre of mass. The shape is a BilobedEllipsoid.

Exact silhouettes. The silhouette of two ellipsoids is exactly the union of their two projected ellipses, so each event constrains the shape without approximation. Rotational amplitudes and phases come from the area of the union over a rotation.

Volume. The volume (and volume_equivalent_radius) counts the overlap of the lobes once.

Output. ellipsoid_mesh(shape) writes the fitted body as an OBJ file, and occview shows it in 3-D.

Fitting strategy

Composite fits can have several minima. Which contacts belong to which body, mirror solutions and the orientation of the axis are all ambiguous. These help:

  • labels on the contacts where the assignment is clear;
  • the default multistarts (starts=8); for 3-D models, a grid of starting poles (see the 3-D example);
  • hard negative chords and the visible segments, which exclude many minima;
  • distinct solutions are best compared side by side by their χ². The ensemble sampler does not move between separated modes.

Plots and maps

plot(fit) and plot(posterior) draw:

  • the union of the outlines;
  • a cross at the reference centre, and small crosses at the centres of the components;
  • each drop of the chords.

The plot box covers the whole system.

Uncertainty band. The bands of single bodies are built in polar coordinates around one centre, which fails for a non-star-shaped limb. For composite outlines the band is the occupancy of the drawn limbs: the region covered by between 15.9 % and 84.1 % of the limbs drawn from the covariance or the posterior (for nsigma=1). It is labelled "occupancy" in the legend.

Maps. On maps (occ_map, geomap) the shadow path of a composite body spans its whole extent across the track. For a separated satellite this includes the gap between the bodies.
