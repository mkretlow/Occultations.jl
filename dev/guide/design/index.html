Package design and conventions

Architecture

The package is organised in layers; each depends only on the ones above it.

LayerContents
core/Epoch (UTC, two-part JD via ERFA), angles, constants
astro/Star, ephemerides (EphemTable, EphemKeplerian, EphemKernel, EphemPlane), Body, observers (Observer, Spacecraft), fundamental-plane geometry, shadow paths, predictions
body/BodyFrame (pole, rotation), 3-D shapes (Ellipsoid, TriMesh), their projections, Outlines (EllipseOutline, PolarOutline): the apparent limb with crossings, normals, areas
fit/the generic inference engine: Params and priors, fit (multistart Levenberg–Marquardt, covariance, error propagation), profile/confidence_interval, sample (ensemble MCMC)
photometry/LightCurve (immutable), diffraction model, Atmosphere ray tracing, TimingProblem
event/Contact, Chord, Event, and Tracks: each observer's path (f, g)(t) from the full reduction, stored as Chebyshev polynomials (≪ 1 m), so that fits never touch ephemerides
limb/LimbModels and the LimbProblem (contacts, negative chords, light curves, amplitudes, clock offsets) and their results
plots/Plots recipes; maps with GeoMakie (geomap, module GeoMaps); Makie mesh viewer (module MeshViews). ext/ (package extensions, loaded with the optional package): HTTP services, SPICE

Every fit is an AbstractProblem (parameters + normalised residuals), so all of them share the same solver, uncertainties and Bayesian sampler: a single light-curve timing fit, a 2-D limb fit, a joint light-curve/atmosphere fit and a multi-event 3-D fit are used identically. Results are returned, never stored in the inputs.

Conventions

  • Occultation is an alias of Event (one occultation: star, body, reference epoch, chords).
  • Times: Epoch (UTC); light curves in seconds from their tref; TDB internally.
  • Fundamental plane: (f, g) is the position of the star relative to the body (ephemeris) centre seen by the observer, in km at the body, f towards east, g towards north. A fitted centre (f₀, g₀) is the offset of the body from its ephemeris; astrometry converts it to Δα cos δ = f₀/D, Δδ = g₀/D.
  • Position angles from north towards east; an ellipse's position_angle is that of its minor axis (the apparent pole of an oblate body).
  • Body frame: IAU convention (Archinal et al., 2018) (pole α, δ; prime meridian W = W0 + 360°·24/P·d), right-handed.
