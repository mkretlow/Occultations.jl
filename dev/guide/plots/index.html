Plots and maps

Limb fit

plot(fit; event=1, uncertainty=:region, numbers=true). The fundamental plane as seen from the Earth: f towards east (drawn to the left, as on the sky), g towards north, in km at the body, relative to the ephemeris position. The black curve is the fitted limb and the cross its centre (the offset from the ephemeris, i.e. the astrometry). Coloured segments are the occulted parts of the positive chords, with dotted continuations; dashed gray lines are negative chords. The number at each chord is its index in the legend (colours repeat). The red segments at the chord ends ("contact-time uncertainty") are ±1σ of the contact times, converted to km with the shadow velocity: a limb passing within them fits that contact.

The gray area shows the uncertainty of the limb. There are three kinds; the keywords are described in Uncertainty of the limb in the plots. By default (uncertainty=:shape), the gray band is the 1σ shape band, and the small black ellipse at the centre cross is the 1σ error ellipse of the centre.

The limb can be compared with the chords in three ways:

  • the limb should pass through the red segments;
  • no negative chord should cross it;
  • where no chord constrains the limb, the region widens and can be asymmetric. For example, if only the southern limb is observed, the size and centre trade off against each other.

The fitted dimensions with 1σ errors (a, b and D_eq of an ellipse; R and D of a circle; median −/+ for a posterior) are printed in the right margin below the legend (dimensions=false omits them).

Posterior limbs

plot(posterior). The same view and the same uncertainty displays, computed from limbs drawn from the MCMC posterior instead of the fit covariance. The black curve is the maximum-posterior limb.

Corner plot

cornerplot(posterior; vars, axislabels, truths):

  • Diagonal: the marginal posterior of each parameter. The title gives the median with the 16 % and 84 % quantiles as −/+ errors (dashed lines).
  • Off-diagonal: the joint posterior of a pair: samples, with contours enclosing 39 % and 86 % of the probability. These are the 1σ and 2σ regions of a 2-D Gaussian.
  • The star marks the maximum-posterior sample. Reference values (truths, e.g. a publication) are drawn in orange: a vertical line in the histograms, a diamond in the 2-D panels; the legend names them (truth_label).
  • The title is the name of the event (plot_title sets another).
  • The legend panel gives the number of samples and walkers, the acceptance fraction and the autocorrelation time τ. Sampling is reliable if the chain length is much greater than 50τ.
  • Elongated or curved contours show correlations and degeneracies (e.g. size against aspect). A reference value outside the 86 % contour disagrees with the fit.

3-D posterior histograms

Marginal posteriors of a triaxial ellipsoid (a ≥ b ≥ c, rotating about c), fitted to a single occultation with the pole unknown (isotropic prior). The two histograms compare two sets of constraints:

  • occultation + amplitude (blue):

    • the chords fix the apparent ellipse at one instant;
    • the light-curve amplitude Δm fixes the elongation the body shows over a rotation, √[(a²cos²ψ + c²sin²ψ)/(b²cos²ψ + c²sin²ψ)].

    Together they do not determine a, b, c and the pole. One apparent ellipse can be cut from many ellipsoids, seen at different aspects ψ and rotational orientations. The least-squares fits form a valley of equally good solutions, and the posterior spreads along it. The valley includes long, thin bodies seen nearly end-on, which give the tail to large diameters.

  • + rotational phase (orange): adds the phase of the light curve at the occultation time, i.e. where the body was in its rotation (0.63 rotations after maximum light for 2003 UY117). The phase says how far the long axis was from the sky plane during the occultation, and removes the valley solutions in which the chords cut the body in a different orientation.

How to read the panels:

  • Left, volume-equivalent diameter D_eq,V (the diameter of a sphere of the same volume, 2(abc)^⅓): the phase cuts the tail of large diameters. For 2003 UY117 the median moves from 243 −24 +33 km to 241 −21 +20 km. With hard negative chords, most of the very elongated solutions are already excluded by the occultation itself. The paper's 235 ± 25 km (dashed line) agrees.
  • Right, aspect angle: the angle between the spin axis and the line of sight (90° = equator-on, the full light-curve amplitude; 0° = pole-on, no amplitude).
    • A pole flip (ψ → 180° − ψ) leaves the occultation and the amplitude unchanged, so only min(ψ, 180° − ψ) is determined, and that is what is plotted.
    • The amplitude alone sets a lower bound on the aspect. Even a very elongated body must be seen far enough from pole-on to produce Δm = 0.36 mag.
    • The phase does not narrow the aspect (2003 UY117: 71 −19 +14° without, 69 −16 +13° with the phase; paper 70°). Its effect is on the shape and size.
    • The sampler visits the two mirror poles unequally: 66 % and 59 % of the samples are in one hemisphere, instead of 50 %. Folding the aspect removes this sampling artefact.
  • The posterior of a single event is wide in any case: only further occultations at other rotational phases, or a pole from photometry, determine a, b and c individually. A narrow peak would indicate a constraint (or a prior) that is stronger than the data.

Maps

geomap(occ_map(...)) (GeoMakie):

  • Shadow path: the black lines are the shadow limits and the blue line is the centre line. Red dashed lines are offsets outside the shadow (e.g. 1σ of the ephemeris). The blue dots are time marks along the centre line, every marks s.
  • Night side: on a globe (orthographic projection, the default of occ_map) the Earth's limb is drawn as a circle, the night side is shaded transparently (with the twilight, Sun 0–18° below the horizon, as a gradient) and the terminator is drawn dash-dotted. night and terminator switch them on or off for any projection.
  • Stations: positive stations are green circles and negative ones red crosses, numbered as in the legend (observer_labels=:name writes the names on the map).
  • geomap adds the shaded shadow band, country borders and capitals.
  • Prediction and post-fit path:
    • occ_map(event; error_mas=20) is the prediction from the ephemeris alone, with the nominal radius and the cross-track ephemeris uncertainty (mas) as ±1σ lines;
    • occ_map(fit) is the reconstruction after the event: the fitted centre, the limits as the limb's extent across the motion, and their 1σ from the fit;
    • geomap(prediction, fit) draws both, so the shift of the shadow relative to the prediction (the astrometric offset) can be seen at a glance. overlays, overlay_labels, overlay_colors and path_label add further paths;
    • the post-fit 1σ lines are much closer to the limits than those of a prediction: after the event the chords fix the shadow to kilometres.

Mesh views

meshview(shape) shows a shape model in its body frame with the x, y and z axes (z = spin axis). meshview(body, t) shows the body as seen from the Earth at time t, with its projected limb and the position angle of the pole.
