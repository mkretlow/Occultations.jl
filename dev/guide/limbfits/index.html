Limb fits

Measurement model

  • Contacts: by default the residual of a contact is the distance along the chord between the observed and the predicted contact (timing residual × speed), with uncertainty √((σ_t |v|)² + σ_limb²) (σ_limb: an allowance for limb topography and unmodelled timing errors; fit_limb(...; σ_limb=:auto) sets it so that χ²/dof = 1). This is the measurement model: a grazing chord, whose timing hardly constrains the radius, gets its proper weight, and the residual stays finite at tangency; a chord that misses the limb is penalised by the distance by which it misses. residual=:radial gives SORA's radial residuals; the difference is explained in Residuals along the chords or radial residuals?.
  • Negative chords: negative=:hard (default) forbids a limb crossing the observed track (zero posterior probability); :soft penalises the depth of the crossing, in units of sensitivity × speed; :none ignores negative chords.
  • Light curves (photometry=true): the flux of every light curve is modelled directly from the limb (predicted contacts and velocities normal to the limb, diffraction, stellar disc, passband, exposure) or from the atmosphere; the flux levels are profiled. This needs no separate timing step and handles grazes, partial and negative light curves uniformly.
  • Clock offsets: fit_dt=["station", …] fits dt_station (true = recorded + dt).
  • Priors: priors=Dict(:oblateness => (0.1, 0.02)), fix=[...], init=Dict(...), bounds=Dict(...), or setparam! on a problem.

Every event has its own centre (center_f_k, center_g_k: offset of the body from its ephemeris in the fundamental plane), so multi-event fits are not biased by ephemeris errors. A new model only has to declare its parameters and return an Outline per event.

# three events of a rotating triaxial body plus a light-curve amplitude
amp = RotationalAmplitude("2023-06-01", 0.31, 0.02)          # Δm ± σ
phs = RotationalPhase(ev1.tref, 0.63, 0.05)                   # rotations since maximum light ± σ
r3 = fit(LimbProblem([ev1, ev2, ev3]; model=EllipsoidLimb(principal=true), amplitudes=[amp],
                     phases=[phs], priors=Dict(:pole_dec => isotropic_pole())))
r3[:a], r3[:b], r3[:c], r3[:volume_equivalent_radius], r3[:apparent_a_2], r3[:aspect_2]

# a shape model
mesh = read_obj("shape.obj"; scale=1.0)                       # km
rm = fit_limb([ev1, ev2]; model=MeshLimb(mesh; fit_orientation=true), fix=[:pole_ra, :pole_dec])

The classical formulas of Dunham et al. (2019) and Magnusson (1986) for the apparent axes, and the rotational amplitude of a triaxial ellipsoid, are available (projected_axes_dunham, projected_axes_magnusson, rotational_amplitude); the package uses the exact projection (project), which agrees with both to machine precision.

Derivatives

Fits use automatic differentiation (ForwardDiff) for their Jacobians:

  • where it applies: limb fits from contacts and negative chords (ellipse, circle, 3-D ellipsoid) and timing fits;
  • it gives exact derivatives, and is faster (≈ 3× for an ellipse fit);
  • joint light-curve, atmosphere and mesh fits fall back to central finite differences automatically;
  • fit(problem; ad=false) forces finite differences.

Limb fit or posterior?

Both use the same likelihood: contacts, negative chords, light curves and constraints. They differ in how they describe the uncertainty.

  • Limb fit (fit_limb, least squares):
    • it finds the best-fitting parameters and approximates the uncertainty by a Gaussian around them, from the covariance (the curvature of χ² at the minimum);
    • this is exact for a linear model with Gaussian errors, and fast;
    • it fails where χ² is not a paraboloid: few chords, a limb constrained on one side only, parameters at a bound, hard constraints (negative chords, a ≥ b ≥ c), or degeneracies such as a mirror solution or the 3-D valley;
    • the 68.3 % region of the fit plot is drawn from this Gaussian, with the draws that violate a hard constraint rejected (a truncated Gaussian).
  • Posterior (sample, ensemble MCMC):
    • it explores the actual probability distribution of the parameters, including the priors (e.g. an isotropic pole, clock-offset priors);
    • it is slower (thousands of model evaluations);
    • it is the reliable description whenever the two disagree: asymmetric errors, banana- shaped correlations, multiple solutions.
  • The plots differ in their black curve: the best fit, or the maximum-posterior sample. With flat priors these coincide up to sampling noise.

If the regions and the errors of both plots agree, the Gaussian approximation is adequate.

Negative chords

Negative chords are hard constraints by default (negative = :hard): a limb that crosses the track of a negative chord is excluded from the fit, the 68.3 % region and the posterior. The option negative = :soft accepts a crossing with a penalty, scaled by sensitivity × speed. It suits a chord whose detection limit is uncertain, where a short event within one long exposure or in noisy data could have been missed. The region can still touch a negative chord where many admissible limbs pass close to it.
