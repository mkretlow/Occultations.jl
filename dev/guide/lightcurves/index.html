Light curves and timing

Reading light curves

lc = read_pymovie("pymovie.csv")                        # date from the header (or date="YYYY-MM-DD")
lc = read_tangra("tangra.csv"; date="2024-01-10")        # SignalMinusBackground (1)
lc = read_astroimagej("measurements.tbl"; target=1)      # JD_UTC, rel_flux_T1 ± rel_flux_err_T1
lc = read_lightcurve("lc.txt"; timescale=:jd, cols=(1, 2, 3))   # generic text file

read_lightcurve reads generic text files; the readers for PyMovie, Tangra and AstroImageJ take the columns and the date from the files of these programs. normalize, select, detect_occultation and simulate_lightcurve prepare, cut, search and simulate light curves (see the API reference).

Fitting the contact times

fit_timing(lc; vel, dist, star_diam) fits the immersion and emersion times with a physical model of the light curve: Fresnel diffraction integrated over the passband, the projected stellar disc and the exposure time, with the flux levels profiled (fitted at each step by linear least squares). The result has the covariance of the times; sample gives their posterior. Short (grazing) events and partial transparency are part of the model.

Reading the timing-fit plot

plot(fit_timing(lc; ...)):

  • The light curve (points) with the model, which includes diffraction, the stellar diameter and the exposure.
  • Dotted vertical lines mark the fitted immersion and emersion times.
  • Residuals are shown below, offset; they should scatter without structure. Systematic wiggles at the contacts point to a wrong velocity, stellar diameter or exposure time.

Double stars

When the occulted star is double, the stations see the occultation of either component, with different depths, or of both. The light curves are then fitted directly with a model of two point sources:

r = fit_limb(ev; photometry=true, double_star=true,
             init=Dict(:star_f1 => 0.75, :star_x1 => -300.0, :star_y1 => -200.0, …))
r[:star_f1], r[:star_sep_mas], r[:star_pa], r[:star_dmag]
post = sample(r)                                      # the uncertainties

The primary carries the fraction star_f1 of the flux and lies at (star_x1, star_y1) [km, in f and g at the body] from the photocentre, the secondary at −f₁/(1 − f₁)·(x₁, y₁). Each is occulted by the same limb, with diffraction, the stellar disc and the exposure. Derived are the separation (star_sep_km, star_sep_mas), the position angle of the secondary from the primary and the magnitude difference. As every joint fit of light curves, it needs a start near the solution (e.g. from the depths and times of the drops) and its uncertainties are best taken from the posterior. See the 2007 OC10 example.
