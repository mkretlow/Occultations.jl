Occultations.jl compared with published reductions

Twenty-three published occultation studies were re-reduced with Occultations.jl from the timings, stations and stars given in the papers (2002 XV93 and 2007 OC10: from the authors' light curves) (DOIs in references/). The examples, their data and their complete printed output are listed below, grouped by the kind of analysis (as in the overview of the examples): first the cases the package reproduces, then those with differences from the publication.

Common setup and caveats

  • Input: the published ingress/egress times and their 1σ errors, the station coordinates and the occulted star (position at the event epoch, angular diameter). The original light curves are not public, so the timing step of the reduction is not repeated.
  • Ephemerides: the current JPL Horizons solution (stored in examples/data), while the papers used older JPL orbits (JPL#28, #18, #20). The fitted centres (offsets from the ephemeris) therefore differ from the published ones; shapes, sizes and the absolute astrometric positions do not depend on this.
  • Negative chords: the observed intervals are not tabulated in the papers; ±5–6 min around the event were assumed. Negative chords are hard constraints (the package default); the exposure (plus dead) time is stored as the chord's sensitivity for the optional soft constraint.
  • Fits: residuals along the chords (the package default) and radial residuals (as SORA and most papers). Uncertainties from the covariance matrix, χ² profiles and posterior sampling; where χ²/dof ≫ 1 they are scaled or a limb allowance σ_limb is fitted.
  • Position angles: the package gives the position angle of the minor axis, measured from north towards +f (east). The UY117 paper uses the same convention (from north, clockwise with f to the right); the angle of the KX14 paper agrees with that of the major axis.

Summary

  • Nine of these fits were repeated with SORA on the same data; the two packages agree (see Occultations.jl and SORA).

  • The package reproduces the published geometry (distances, velocities, stellar diameters, Fresnel scales) of the events, and the published limbs within their uncertainties in most cases. Exceptions: the 2002 KX14 minor axis and diameter (≈ 2σ larger with a joint multi-event fit) and the 2014 position angle of 2003 AZ84 (10° larger).

  • Most data sets show more scatter than the timing errors (χ²/dof 8–230 for KX14 and Huya, 13 for 2002 TC302); limb allowances, clock-offset priors, per-event centres and posterior sampling make this explicit instead of hiding it in the fitting method.

  • Degeneracies are exposed rather than resolved by a choice of starting point: the mirror solution of a two-chord event (KX14 D), the shape–pole valley of a single event with a light-curve amplitude (UY117), the mirror ring planes of Haumea, and the unconstrained long axes of Makemake and the three-chord ellipses (2003 AZ84, 2007 UK126). Posteriors show errors several times those from one-dimensional χ² cuts in these cases.

  • Issues found in the publications: the Occ. D timings of KX14 (since corrected by the corrigendum), an inconsistent stellar diameter for KX14 event E, a sign error in the latitude of Apache Point, the use of the Gaia DR2 star position for the UY117 astrometry, a sign error in the 2012 RA offset of 2003 AZ84, the J2000 star position of 2002 TC302 (0.64″ from the star at the event), and misprinted station or star coordinates (2003 AZ84, 2003 VS2).
