Occultations.jl and SORA: the fitting methods compared

SORA (Stellar Occultation Reduction and Analysis; Gomes-Júnior et al. (2022), MNRAS 511, 1167, doi:10.1093/mnras/stac032; arXiv:2201.01799) is the Python package used for most of the published reductions reproduced in Comparison with published reductions.

This document compares the fitting methods of the two packages: limb fits, shape-model fits, negative chords, light-curve timing, and the uncertainties and plots derived from them. The description of SORA is based on its source code: the master branch of github.com/riogroup/SORA, read on 2026-11-02. The relevant modules are occultation/fitting.py, occultation/utils.py, extra/chisquare.py and lightcurve/core.py. Earlier versions, used by older papers, may differ in details. SORA 0.3.3 was also run on nine of the published events and on a speed benchmark, with the same input as Occultations.jl (sections The same events reduced with both packages and Computing time).

Ellipse fit

SORA – fit_ellipse

  • Data: the contact points of the positive chords. The uncertainty of a point is its timing error × |v|, plus ellipse_error (km) in quadrature.
  • Residual: radial. The model point lies on the ray from the ellipse centre through the data point, and χ² is the sum of the squared distances between the data points and these model points, divided by (σᵢ² + ellipse_error²).
  • Parameters: centre (f, g), equatorial radius a, oblateness ε, position angle. Each is searched within a window value ± dvalue; a zero width fixes the parameter.
  • Search (method):
    • 'least_squares' (the default): trust-region least squares, then a Monte Carlo refinement (fastchi) around the solution; the least-squares stage minimises Σ r⁴ instead of χ² (see below);
    • 'differential_evolution': the same, with differential evolution as the first stage;
    • 'chisqr' / 'fastchi': a pure Monte Carlo search with uniform random draws in the windows, until number_chi samples are collected.
  • Result (ChiSquare.get_nsigma(sigma=sigma_result)):
    • all samples with χ² < χ²_min + σ² are selected;
    • for each parameter, the result is the midpoint of the range of the selected samples, and the uncertainty is half that range.

Occultations.jl – fit_limb

  • Residual: along the chord by default (residual=:chord). This is the measurement model: the timing residual × |v|, with σ = √((σₜ|v|)² + σ_limb²). A chord that misses the limb is penalised by the distance of the miss. residual=:radial gives SORA's definition.
  • Search: Levenberg–Marquardt with multiple starts, a Jacobian by automatic differentiation, and the parameters reduced to a canonical form.
  • Result:
    • the minimum of χ², with errors from the covariance (JᵀJ)⁻¹;
    • likelihood profiles (profile);
    • the MCMC posterior (sample): the median and the 15.87–84.13 % quantiles of the marginal distribution.
  • Derived quantities (b, Req, Deq, astrometry, …) are propagated with the covariance or taken from the posterior.

Differences that matter when comparing numbers

  1. The definition of the uncertainty. SORA's "1σ" is half the range of the parameter among all sampled solutions with Δχ² < 1.
    • This is a likelihood-profile interval for one parameter at a time (Δχ² = 1). It is correct for a single parameter of a linear, Gaussian problem.
    • It is estimated from the extremes of a finite random sample. Few samples near the edges make it too small, and it depends on the windows d*, number_chi and the random draws.
    • The reported value is the midpoint of the interval, not the χ² minimum. For an asymmetric χ² surface, this differs from the best fit.
    • Errors of quantities derived afterwards (e.g. ε from a and b, R_eq) are not always consistent with those of a and b. Examples are 1995 SM55 and Ixion in Comparison with published reductions.
  2. Radial residuals understate the misfit of oblique and grazing contacts by cos θ (see Residuals along the chords or radial residuals?). χ²/dof is therefore smaller with radial residuals (2003 UY117: 0.41 radial vs 2.10 along the chords).
  3. No scaling with χ². With χ²/dof ≫ 1, SORA's Δχ² < 1 intervals are taken at face value. The 2019 refit of Huya in Rommel et al. (2025) has a′ ± 0.11 km at χ²/dof ≈ 211. Here, σ_limb = :auto, scale_errors = true or clock-offset priors make the excess scatter explicit.

  • The same χ². SORA's formula evaluated at the Occultations.jl solution gives the same χ², to < 0.1 % where the contact errors are ≳ 1 km. For 1995 SM55 (contact errors ≈ 0.15 km) it differs by 2 %: the contact points of the two packages differ by up to ≈ 0.1 km, which is negligible elsewhere but not at this precision.
  • SORA's least squares minimises Σ r⁴. Its residual function for the ellipse (ellipseError in occultation/fitting.py) returns the squared normalised residuals, ((Δf)² + (Δg)²)/σ², and scipy.optimize.least_squares squares them again. Minimising Σ r⁴ with scipy from the same start reproduces SORA's least-squares χ² in every case (columns "SORA least squares" and "scipy, Σ r⁴"), while Σ r² gives the Occultations.jl minimum. The least-squares solution therefore has a higher χ² than the minimum (Varda 8.76 vs 7.46, 2002 GZ32 52.9 vs 43.3, 1995 SM55 457 vs 353); only for the circle of Makemake are the two nearly the same. The residual of the light-curve fit (_occ_model_fitError in lightcurve/core.py, occ_lcfit(method='least_squares')) has the same form. The master branch of 2026-08-03 has the same code.
  • The Monte Carlo stage samples around the least-squares solution and mostly repairs its bias, but does not reach the minimum where χ² is steep: for 1995 SM55 only 2 of 2000 samples lie within Δχ² < 1, so SORA reports ε′ = 0.230 ± 0.000 and a′ ± 0.4 km. Errors of this size are published for this event (ε′ = 0.200 ± 0.002); with χ²/dof ≈ 40 they are not meaningful. With a limb allowance (σ_limb = 13.5 km, χ²/dof = 1) both packages give errors fifty times larger.
  • SORA's random draws are not seeded; its Monte Carlo results change slightly from run to run.

Setting up SORA for these runs needed care: with EphemPlanete (a local table) SORA's default chord projection ignores the observer and puts every station on the geocentre, so the scripts use the older "geocenter" projection (occ.chords._method = "geocenter"); and Star(…, local=True) still queries VizieR unless nomad=False.

Speed matters where fits are repeated: posteriors, χ² profiles, joint light-curve fits, multi-event 3-D fits, and coverage tests such as those of the synthetic examples, which refit hundreds of simulated events.

Negative chords

SORA – filter_negative_chord(chord, chisquare, step=1, sigma=0). After the fit, each accepted solution is tested against one negative chord:

  • the chord's full path is sampled every step seconds (step='exposure' samples the exposures of its light curve);
  • points farther than 1.5 a from the centre are ignored;
  • the solution is kept only if every remaining point lies outside the ellipse, by more than −sigma km.

The filter returns a reduced ChiSquare, from which the results are recomputed. It has to be applied to each negative chord explicitly, and the minimisation itself does not know about negative chords.

Occultations.jl. Negative chords are part of the likelihood:

  • :hard: a limb crossing the observed track is inadmissible (χ² = ∞, zero posterior);
  • :soft: a penalty scaled by the exposure × speed.

The best fit, the covariance, the profiles and the posterior all respect them. check_negative_chords(fit) lists the distance of each negative chord from the limb.

The examples show why this matters. Rommel et al. (2025) filtered the 2023 June 24 Huya solutions with the Belesta negative chord only; their free solution crosses the Guirguillano negative chord by 41 km (see Comparison with published reductions).

Shape models

SORA – fit_shape(occ; center_f, dcenter_f, center_g, dcenter_g, scale, dscale, loop, sigma_result, model_error)

  • The orientation is fixed: the limb of the body's shape model at the time of closest approach (occ.body.shape.get_limb).
  • A Monte Carlo search (loop draws) fits only the centre and the scale, with radial residuals (fit_to_limb).
  • One occultation at a time.

Occultations.jl – MeshLimb(mesh; fit_orientation=true)

  • Fits the centre, the scale and the orientation: pole, W0, and optionally an in-plane rotation per event.
  • Uses all events at once, each with its own light-time corrected rotation phase.
  • The triaxial-ellipsoid model (EllipsoidLimb) adds rotational light-curve amplitudes and phases as constraints. occview shows the result in 3-D, with the chords and the rotational light curve.

Light-curve timing

SORA – occ_lcfit

  • Model: the Fresnel diffraction of an opaque (or partly transparent) edge, averaged over the two edges of the passband, the stellar disc and the exposure time.
  • Flux levels: fixed from the event detection (occ_detect), not fitted.
  • Search: a Monte Carlo search of immersion and emersion times by default ('chisqr'), within ±delta_t. Least squares or differential evolution followed by Monte Carlo are options.
  • Errors: half the range of the solutions with Δχ² < σ², as for the ellipse.

Occultations.jl – fit_timing

  • Model: diffraction integrated over the passband (sampled), the stellar disc and the exposure.
  • Flux levels: profiled, i.e. the linear least-squares solution at each step, so they are fitted rather than fixed.
  • Errors: Levenberg–Marquardt with automatic differentiation, a covariance, and an optional posterior (sample) for asymmetric or non-Gaussian timing errors.
  • Joint fits: with fit_limb(…; photometry=true) the light curves are fitted together with the limb, and with an atmosphere if given. No separate timing step is needed, and grazes, partial and negative light curves are treated in the same way.

Uncertainty regions in the figures

In SORA-based papers, the gray "1σ uncertainty region" around the fitted ellipse is drawn from the solutions with χ² < χ²_min + 1 ("3σ" with + 9). It is the envelope of the ellipses compatible with the data for one parameter at a time.

The corresponding display in Occultations.jl is uncertainty=:pointwise (with nsigma=3 for "3σ"); the examples write it as *_limb_fit_paper.png. Its default display (uncertainty=:shape) and the 68.3 % region of whole limbs (uncertainty=:region) are explained in Uncertainty of the limb in the plots. For 2003 UY117, a whole limb stays inside the pointwise band only 7 % of the time. The 68.3 % region is about as wide as SORA's "3σ" envelope.

Both packages reduce the same geometry: tracks, the fundamental plane, Fresnel diffraction and astrometry. On the same data the best-fitting limbs agree (see The same events reduced with both packages), and Occultations.jl is two to three orders of magnitude faster (see Computing time). The differences lie in the uncertainties, the treatment of negative chords and the joint 3-D and photometric models.
