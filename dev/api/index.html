API reference

All exported functions and types, grouped by topic. The package module:

Occultations — Module
Occultations

Reduction and analysis of stellar occultations by solar-system bodies: light-curve timing with diffraction and atmosphere models, limb fits of 2-D ellipses, 3-D ellipsoids and shape meshes, binary systems, contact binaries and ring systems, over one or several events (with negative chords, clock offsets and rotational-amplitude constraints), uncertainties by covariance, χ² profiles and Bayesian posterior sampling, astrometry, predictions and maps.

Layers:

  • astrometry (Epoch, Star, ephemerides, observers, fundamental-plane geometry)
  • bodies (frames, 3-D shapes, projected Outlines)
  • photometry (LightCurve, diffraction, Atmosphere, timing fits)
  • events (Chord, Event, cached Tracks)
  • limb models (EllipseLimb, CircleLimb, EllipsoidLimb, MeshLimb, BinaryLimb, ContactBinaryLimb, ContactBinaryEllipsoids, RingedLimb) and LimbProblem
  • a generic inference engine (fit, sample, profile) used by all problems

Time and angles

Occultations.Epoch — Type
Epoch

An instant of time in UTC, stored as an ERFA two-part quasi Julian Date (jd1 + jd2), which keeps sub-microsecond resolution and handles leap seconds.

Constructors

Epoch("2020-06-03T12:30:45.1234")   # ISO string (UTC); 'T' or space separator
Epoch(DateTime(2020, 6, 3, 12))     # Dates.DateTime (UTC)
Epoch(2459004.02; scale=:utc)       # Julian date in :utc, :tt or :tdb
Epoch(2020, 6, 3, 12, 30, 45.1234)  # calendar components (UTC)

Arithmetic: e + 1.5 (seconds, SI), e2 - e1 (seconds, SI), comparisons.

Examples

julia> t = Epoch("2024-01-10T15:00:00");

julia> t + 1.5
Epoch("2024-01-10T15:00:01.5000")

julia> round(Epoch("2024-01-10 15:00:30") - t; digits=6)
30.0

julia> round(Epoch("2017-01-01T00:00:00") - Epoch("2016-12-31T23:59:59"); digits=6)   # leap second
2.0

Ephemerides and bodies

Occultations.AbstractEphemeris — Type
AbstractEphemeris

Supertype of all ephemerides. A concrete ephemeris E must implement

_geocentric_vector(eph::E, t::Epoch) -> Vector{Float64}

returning the astrometric (light-time corrected) geocentric position of the body in km, ICRS axes. User offsets (see add_offset!) are applied by geocentric_vector.

Every ephemeris has fields offset_ra, offset_dec [mas].

Occultations.Body — Type
Body(name, ephem; radius=NaN, diameter=NaN, H=NaN, G=0.15, pole=nothing,
     rotation_period=NaN, W0=0, frame=nothing, shape=nothing, GM=NaN, density=NaN)

An occulting body: an ephemeris plus physical parameters.

  • radius – reference (equivalent) radius [km] (or give diameter), used for predictions and as the default starting value of limb fits
  • H, G – absolute magnitude and slope (apparent_magnitude)
  • pole = (ra, dec) [deg], rotation_period [h] (negative: retrograde) and W0 (prime meridian at J2000 TDB) define the body frame (BodyFrame) unless frame is given
  • shape – a 3-D shape (Ellipsoid or TriMesh) in the body frame
  • GM [km³/s²] or density [kg/m³] – needed for atmosphere models

Occultations.EphemKeplerian — Type
EphemKeplerian(; a, e, i, Ω, ω, M, epoch, name="")

Heliocentric two-body ephemeris from osculating elements referred to the J2000 ecliptic: semimajor axis a [au], eccentricity e, inclination i, longitude of node Ω, argument of perihelion ω, mean anomaly M [deg] at epoch (an Epoch, or a TDB Julian date). Occultations.EphemPlane — Type
EphemPlane(star, epoch, x, y; dist, units=:km, name="")
EphemPlane(star, epoch, x0, y0, dx, dy; dist, units=:km, name="")

Ephemeris of an occulting body given only by its shadow on the fundamental plane, as in the Besselian elements of an occultation prediction: the shadow axis (body centre) is at

x(τ) = x[1] + x[2] τ + x[3] τ² + …,   y(τ) likewise,   τ = t − epoch,

relative to the geocentre, in the plane through the geocentre perpendicular to the direction of star, x towards east and y towards north (so the geocentric (f, g) of positionv are (−x, −y)). With units=:km the coefficients are in km, km/s, km/s², …; with units=:earth_radii in Earth radii, Earth radii/hour, … (CORA, Occult). dist is the geocentric distance of the body [km] (needed for the light time, the Fresnel scale and the angular conversion of the astrometry).

The body is placed so that the occultation of star (the same Star must be used for the Occultation) has exactly this geometry; topocentric observers, maps, light curves and limb fits then work as with any other ephemeris. The predicted path is reproduced, not the body's orbit: use it only near epoch. See parse_cora for CORA predictions.

Occultations.BodyFrame — Type
BodyFrame(; pole_ra, pole_dec, W0=0, rotation_rate=0, epoch=JD_J2000,

Body-fixed frame in the IAU convention (Archinal et al. 2018): the pole is at α = pole_ra + pole_ra_rate T, δ = pole_dec + pole_dec_rate T and the prime meridian at W = W0 + rotation_rate d, with d days and T Julian centuries (TDB) since epoch (a TDB Julian date). Angles in degrees, rotation_rate in deg/day (negative for retrograde rotators), pole rates in deg/century.

The frame is right-handed and longitudes are planetocentric, positive towards east.

Occultations.Atmosphere — Type
Atmosphere(gas=:N2; T0=40, dTdz=0, z_inv=0, z_fmf=Inf, μ, K, ztop=NaN)

Spherically symmetric, transparent atmosphere in hydrostatic equilibrium.

  • gas – :N2, :CH4 or :CO (molecular mass μ [amu] and refractivity K [m³]; both can be given explicitly)
  • temperature: T0 [K] at the surface, rising (or falling) by dTdz [K/km] up to the altitude z_inv [km], isothermal above (a Pluto-like inversion layer; dTdz = 0 gives an isothermal atmosphere)
  • z_fmf [km]: above this altitude the density follows the free-molecular-flow profile n ∝ (R + z)⁻² (low Jeans parameter, Arimatsu et al. 2026)
  • ztop [km]: top of the model (default: 30 scale heights above the inversion)
  • width [km]: the top of the inversion layer and the onset of the free molecular flow are smooth transitions of this width (a kink in the density gradient would produce a spurious jump of the refracted flux, since φ ∝ 1/(1 + D dω/dr))

The free parameter of a fit is the surface pressure (pressure, nbar); the radius comes from the limb model and the gravity from the body (GM or density). See atmosphere_profile, xv93_atmosphere, pluto_atmosphere.

Occultations.LimbProblem — Type
LimbProblem(events; model=EllipseLimb(), residual=:chord, negative=:hard, visible=:auto,
            σ_limb=0, σ_ring=σ_limb, fit_dt=(), photometry=false, atmosphere=nothing, amplitudes=(), phases=(),
            fix=(), init=Dict(), priors=Dict(), bounds=Dict())

Fit of the apparent limb to one event or a vector of events (multi-event fit).

Parameters: the centre of each event (center_f, center_g [km] in the fundamental plane relative to the ephemeris; suffix _k for event k in multi-event fits), the parameters of the model (LimbModel), a clock offset dt_<chord> [s] for every chord in fit_dt, and pressure [nbar] with an atmosphere. Change their settings with fix, init, priors (name => Gaussian(μ, σ) or (μ, σ)), bounds (name => (lo, hi)) or setparam!.

Data:

  • timed contacts of the chords (residual = :chord: distance along the chord between the observed and the predicted contact, i.e. timing residual × speed, which is the measurement model and well behaved for grazing chords; a chord that misses the limb is penalised by the distance by which it misses. :radial: radial distance of the contact point from the limb, as in SORA). Uncertainty per contact: √((σ_t |v|)² + σ_limb²), σ_limb [km] being an allowance for topography; ring contacts take σ_ring instead (default: σ_limb), an allowance for the ring's eccentricity or width variations;
  • negative chords (negative = :hard, the default: limbs crossing the track of a negative chord are excluded; :soft: a penalty proportional to the depth by which the limb crosses the track, in units of sensitivity × speed, for chords whose detection limit is uncertain, e.g. long exposures or low signal-to-noise; :none);
  • the visible segments of the positive chords: where the star was seen before, between and after the drops (shortened at each contact by 3σ of its timing, at least the chord's sensitivity). They are treated as negative chords: visible = :soft (penalty as for negative chords), :hard or :none. The default :auto is :soft for binary and contact-binary models (BinaryLimb, ContactBinaryLimb, ContactBinaryEllipsoids), whose outline can produce a drop where none was seen, and :none otherwise (a convex limb cannot);
  • light curves (photometry = true, or a list of chord names): the light curve itself is fitted with the model flux predicted by the limb — diffraction, stellar diameter, passband, exposure (contacts of these chords are not used separately), or the refractive flux of the atmosphere (Atmosphere) whose surface pressure is then fitted; the flux levels are profiled per light curve;
  • rotational light-curve amplitudes (RotationalAmplitude) and phases (RotationalPhase) for 3-D models.

Solve with fit, sample the posterior with sample.

RotationalAmplitude(epoch, amplitude, σ)

Peak-to-peak amplitude [mag] (± σ) of the rotational light curve observed at epoch, a constraint on the shape and pole of 3-D limb models (geometric scattering: brightness ∝ projected area). Inference: parameters, fits and posteriors

Occultations.AbstractProblem — Type
AbstractProblem

A fitting problem. Implementations provide

parameters(problem) -> Vector{Param}
residuals(problem, values::Vector{Float64}) -> Vector or nothing

(values holds all parameters in the order of parameters; the residuals are normalised by their uncertainties so that χ² = Σ r²; nothing marks an inadmissible point, e.g. a limb crossing a negative chord with hard constraints) and optionally

nobs(problem)                 # number of data points (default: length of the residuals)
derived(problem, values)      # NamedTuple of derived quantities (default: none)

They can then be solved with fit, sampled with sample and profiled with profile.

Examples

A straight line y = a + b x as a problem, with the abscissa x₀ = −a/b where it crosses zero as a derived quantity:

julia> struct Line <: AbstractProblem
           ps::Vector{Param}
           x::Vector{Float64}
           y::Vector{Float64}
           σ::Float64
       end

julia> Occultations.parameters(p::Line) = p.ps

julia> Occultations.residuals(p::Line, v) = (v[1] .+ v[2] .* p.x .- p.y) ./ p.σ

julia> Occultations.derived(p::Line, v) = (x0 = -v[1] / v[2],)

julia> p = Line([Param(:a, 0.0), Param(:b, 1.0)], [0.0, 1, 2, 3], [1.1, 2.9, 5.2, 6.8], 0.1);

julia> r = fit(p);

julia> round.(r[:b]; digits=4)            # (value, σ)
(1.94, 0.0447)

julia> round.(r[:x0]; digits=4)           # derived, with propagated σ
(-0.5619, 0.0541)

julia> round(reduced_chi2(r); digits=3), freenames(r)
(4.1, [:a, :b])

occ_map(star, body, t; window=600, step=5, radius=body.radius, offsets=nothing,

Map of the shadow path between t - window and t + window (computed every step seconds with shadow_path, i.e. on the rotating Earth): central line, body limits (±radius) and optionally error lines (±(radius + error) km), time marks every marks seconds of UTC (whole minutes for marks = 60) along the central line (for an event, error_mas gives the cross-track ephemeris uncertainty of a prediction in mas), observers and the day/night terminator for t. Draw it with geomap.

projection (:ortho, orthographic and centred by default on the sub-stellar point at t, like SORA, or :lonlat) and center ((lon, lat) in degrees) set the default projection of geomap. For an Event the chord observers are shown; for a limb fit the path is that of the fitted centre (ephemeris + fitted offset); the body limits are the extent of the fitted limb across the motion, and the error lines their ±1σ from the fit covariance (centre and shape; ndraw draws).

Occultations.occview — Function
occview(fit_or_posterior; event=1, explore=false, ndraw=24, kwargs...) -> Makie Figure

The occultation in 3-D for a limb fit with a 3-D model (EllipsoidLimb, MeshLimb) or its posterior. The figure has four parts:

  • the body as oriented at the event, seen from the Earth, lit by the Sun (night side dark), with its pole, the Sun direction, the sub-observer and sub-solar points, and the chords in front of it;
  • the silhouette on the sky plane with the chords; for a posterior, ndraw sampled silhouettes and shapes as well;
  • the rotational light curve: the magnitude from the projected area through one rotation, with the phase of the event and the observed amplitudes;
  • sliders for the rotational phase and the event. With explore = true there are also sliders for the pole, W0 and the size, with the live χ² of the problem.

Static with CairoMakie, interactive with GLMakie. See also occanimate.
