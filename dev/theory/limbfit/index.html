The mathematics of the limb fit

All fits minimise χ² = Σ rᵢ² over normalised residuals rᵢ. The sections below define these residuals and how the minimum, the uncertainties and the posterior are obtained.

1. Geometry: the fundamental plane and the tracks

For an observer and a time t, the fundamental-plane coordinates (f, g) [km] give the position of the star relative to the body's ephemeris centre. They are measured perpendicular to the line of sight at the body's distance, f towards east and g towards north. The full reduction (topocentric observer, light time, aberration, proper motion and parallax of the star) is done once per chord. The resulting track (f, g)(t) is stored as Chebyshev polynomials accurate to < 1 m. A fitted clock offset Δtᵢ of chord i shifts its times, t → t + Δtᵢ.

2. The limb

In event k the body's centre is offset from the ephemeris by cₖ = (f₀, g₀)ₖ, the astrometric result. The limb is a closed outline around cₖ, given by its radius r(θ) in each direction:

  • Ellipse (EllipseLimb): semi-major axis a, oblateness ε = 1 − b/a, and the position angle φ of the minor axis (from north towards east). With (u, w) the coordinates of a direction along the major and minor axes, r(θ) = [(u/a)² + (w/b)²]^(−1/2), and the area-equivalent radius is √(ab).
  • Circle (CircleLimb): r = R.
  • Triaxial ellipsoid (EllipsoidLimb): semi-axes a, b, c in the body frame, oriented by the matrix M (ICRS → body) of the pole (α, δ) and the prime-meridian angle W.
    • W(t) = W0 + 360°·(temit − tref)/P, at the light-time corrected emission time of each event.
    • The silhouette is exact. P is a sky basis (east, north, line of sight). The quadratic form Q = Pᵀ Mᵀ diag(a⁻², b⁻², c⁻²) M P is reduced to the sky plane by the Schur complement S = Q₁₂,₁₂ − q qᵀ/Q₃₃, which eliminates the line-of-sight coordinate. The apparent ellipse is xᵀ S x = 1, with semi-axes 1/√λ for the eigenvalues λ of S.
  • Mesh (MeshLimb): the projected vertices are rotated to the event, and each projected edge raises the radius r(θ) in the directions it spans. The result is a polar outline sampled in n directions (the mesh must be star-shaped as seen from its centre); it is scaled by scale and rotated by rotation.

The radial signed distance of a point x (relative to cₖ) from the limb is h(x) = |x| − r(arg x): negative inside, positive outside.

3. Contact residuals

A contact of chord i at the observed time t (error σₜ) lies at xᵢ = (f, g)ᵢ(t + Δtᵢ) − cₖ, where the track moves with velocity v. With a limb allowance σlimb, its uncertainty is σ = √((σₜ |v|)² + σlimb²).

  • Along the chord (residual=:chord): s is the solution of x + s·v on the limb, the entry root for an immersion and the exit root for an emersion.
    • Ellipse: a quadratic in s, A s² + B s + C = 0, solved analytically.
    • Other outlines: bracketing of h along the line, then bisection.
    • The residual is r = −s|v|/σ, i.e. the timing residual × |v|/σ. If the line misses the limb, s is taken at the closest approach and a second residual (distance of the miss)/σ pulls the limb towards the chord.
  • Radial (residual=:radial): r = h(x)/σ. The difference from the residual along the chord is explained in Residuals along the chords or radial residuals?.

4. Negative chords

Along the observed track of a negative chord, d = min h(x) is the closest approach of the track to the limb; d < 0 means the limb crosses the track.

  • :hard (the default): parameters with d < 0 are inadmissible (χ² = ∞, zero posterior).
  • :soft: residual max(−d, 0)/(sensitivity·|v|), where sensitivity is the exposure plus dead time of the chord.

5. Further constraints

Each is a residual (model − observation)/σ:

  • rotational amplitudes: Δm = 2.5 log(a/b) − 1.25 log[(a² cos²ψ + c² sin²ψ)/(b² cos²ψ + c² sin²ψ)], for the aspect ψ of the pole at the epoch of the light curve;
  • rotational phases: the phase since the last maximum of the projected area at the event, wrapped to (−½, ½] of the light-curve period (half a rotation for an ellipsoid);
  • Gaussian priors: (x − μ)/σ. Other priors (LogPrior, e.g. isotropic_pole) enter only the posterior.

6. The minimum: Levenberg–Marquardt with multiple starts

  • Step: δ from (JᵀJ + λ D) δ = −Jᵀr, with D = diag(JᵀJ). λ starts at 10⁻³ and is divided by 5 after a successful step, multiplied by 10 after a failed one.

  • Convergence: relative χ² change < 10⁻⁹, or every |δⱼ| < 10⁻⁷ × the parameter's scale.

  • Jacobian: J = ∂r/∂x by forward-mode automatic differentiation; otherwise central finite differences with steps of 10⁻⁶ × scale (joint light-curve, atmosphere and mesh fits).

  • Starts: the first start is the initial value; the others are drawn uniformly within ±2 × scale around it. An inadmissible start is replaced by a random search nearby. The best χ² wins.

  • Canonical form: the solution is brought to a canonical form, then wrapped:

    ModelCanonical form
    Ellipseε ≥ 0 (otherwise the axes are swapped and φ + 90°), φ mod 180°
    Ellipsoida ≥ b (otherwise swapped and W + 90°), W0 mod 180°
    Periodic parameterswrapped

    It is then polished by a final Levenberg–Marquardt run.

7. Uncertainties of the fit

  • Covariance: C = (JᵀJ)⁻¹ at the minimum (pseudo-inverse if singular). scale_errors = true multiplies it by χ²/dof.
  • Derived quantities (b, Req, Deq, albedo inputs, astrometric offsets, apparent axes of an ellipsoid, …) are propagated linearly: C_d = G C Gᵀ, with G = ∂d/∂x by central differences.
  • Limb allowance: σ_limb = :auto chooses it by bisection so that χ²/dof = 1.
  • Likelihood profile: profile(fit, name) refits all other parameters on a grid of fixed values. Δχ² = 1 gives the 1σ interval, which can be asymmetric.

8. The posterior

  • Density: log p(x) = −χ²(x)/2 + Σ log prior(xⱼ), and −∞ for inadmissible x (hard constraints, bounds).
  • Sampler: the affine-invariant ensemble sampler of Goodman and Weare (2010), as in emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al., 2013):
    • 4n walkers (at least 16; n free parameters) start in a small Gaussian ball around the least-squares solution;
    • each walker moves by the stretch move y = xⱼ + z (x − xⱼ) towards a random other walker xⱼ, with z drawn from g(z) ∝ 1/√z on [1/a, a] (a = 2);
    • the move is accepted with probability min(1, zⁿ⁻¹ p(y)/p(x)).
  • Output: the first third of the steps is burn-in. The integrated autocorrelation time τ (the automatic window of Sokal (1997), c = 5) shows how many independent samples the chain holds. Summaries are the median and the 15.87–84.13 % quantiles.

9. Several events

  • The shape parameters are shared; each event has its own centre cₖ. per_event makes individual shape parameters event-specific, and per-chord clock offsets Δtᵢ can be added.
  • For an ellipsoid or a mesh, each event has its own orientation M(W(tₖ)) and line of sight. Events at different rotational phases and aspects therefore constrain the 3-D shape together.

10. Light curves instead of contacts

With photometry = true, the flux of each light curve is modelled directly:

  • Model: F(t) = fb + (ft − f_b) I(t), where I(t) is the normalised intensity at the observer's position on the track.
  • Airless body: I comes from Fresnel diffraction at the limb, at the predicted contacts and velocities normal to the limb. It is integrated over the passband, the stellar disc and the exposure time.
  • Atmosphere: I is the ray-traced refractive flux (see Atmospheres), multiplied by the diffraction of the surface edge.
  • Flux levels: ft and fb are the linear least-squares solution for given I, so they are profiled out.
  • Residuals: (Fobs − F)/σF, one per data point.
