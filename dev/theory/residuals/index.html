Residuals along the chords or radial residuals?

Both fit the same limb model to the same contact times. They differ in how a contact's misfit is measured and weighted.

Along the chords (residual=:chord, the default) uses the timing measurement itself. For each contact the model predicts the time at which the station's chord crosses the fitted limb.

  • The residual is the distance along the chord between the observed and the predicted contact: the timing residual × the shadow speed.
  • Its uncertainty is σt·v, plus `σlimb` if set.

This is the actual measurement model: a timing error moves the contact point along the chord, and nothing else. So χ² is the correct likelihood of the timings, and the errors and posteriors follow from it. Special cases are handled explicitly:

  • a chord that misses the fitted limb has no predicted contact and is penalised by the distance by which it misses;
  • a grazing chord, whose timing hardly constrains the size, is weighted correctly, and its residual stays finite at tangency.

Radial residuals (residual=:radial, as in SORA and most papers) convert the observed time into a point in the sky plane.

  • The residual is the distance of that point from the limb, measured along the line from the ellipse centre.
  • It is divided by the same σ_t·v.

The error, however, lies along the chord, not along the radius. If the chord meets the limb at an angle θ to the limb normal, a timing error ε moves the point radially by only about v·ε·cos θ. Each normalised residual is therefore understated by the factor cos θ:

  • central chords (cos θ ≈ 1): the two definitions agree;
  • grazing contacts (cos θ → 0): their misfit almost vanishes, however wrong the timing, and they get much less weight.

Consequences:

  • χ² is smaller with radial residuals, so a good radial χ² can hide timing misfits:

  • The parameters usually agree within their errors. They differ where grazing or oblique contacts matter, because those carry different weights (1995 SM55: a′ = 102.9 vs 105.4 km).

  • Errors: a radial fit has no correct likelihood behind it, so its covariance and posterior are only approximate.

Which to use:

  • :chord for results and uncertainties;
  • :radial to reproduce or compare with published fits; the examples print both.
